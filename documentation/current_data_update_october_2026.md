# Official current-data update: October 2026 CPI (2024 base)

**Release:** MoSPI / National Statistical Office, 12 November 2026  
**Reference month:** October 2026 (provisional); September 2026 comparison (final)  
**Source PDF:** https://mospi.gov.in/uploads/latestreleasesfiles/1794480048335-Press%20Release%20of%20CPI%20for%20October%202026%20.pdf

## Purpose and separation from the main estimation data

This is a current-data context update only. It is not appended to the project's 2012-base monthly regression dataset. The October 2026 release belongs to the revised CPI series with base 2024=100. The new series was introduced in February 2026, uses item basket and weights derived primarily from HCES 2023-24, and reorganises the CPI into 12 COICOP 2018 divisions rather than the former six groups. The official release does not create a like-for-like continuation of the older 2012-base series without a documented linking methodology.

## Headline and food aggregates

| Measure | September 2026 (final) | October 2026 (provisional) |
|---|---:|---:|
| CPI General, Combined | 5.54% | 6.09% |
| CFPI, Combined | 7.74% | 8.94% |

The CFPI rate (8.94%) is distinct from the division-level Food and Beverages CPI rate (8.51%). They are different official measures and must not be interchanged or averaged.

## Division-level inflation, October 2026

Combined-sector year-on-year rates from the MoSPI chart are transcribed in `data/external/mospi_october_2026_2024base_divisions.csv`. The highest rates shown are Personal care / social protection / miscellaneous goods and services (10.14%) and Restaurants and accommodation services (10.06%). The lowest are Health (2.05%) and Recreation, sport and culture (2.52%).

## Selected item-level dispersion

`data/external/mospi_october_2026_selected_items.csv` records the five highest and five lowest item inflation rates shown in the release. Examples include onion at 116.22%, garlic at 60.74%, and potato at -14.24%. These are item-level extremes, not representative average food inflation. Silver jewellery (52.39%) is a non-food item example showing that the inflation distribution is heterogeneous outside food as well.

## Interpretation limits

1. These are year-on-year inflation observations at a single endpoint, not dynamic transmission estimates.
2. October 2026 figures are provisional; September 2026 is final.
3. The 2024-base weights, basket and classifications differ from the project's 2012-base historical regression data.
4. The October 2026 release supports an updated descriptive narrative about divergence and relative-price dispersion. It cannot validate or overturn the 2013–2025 regression results.
5. The food division rate (8.51%) and CFPI rate (8.94%) differ and must remain separately labelled.
